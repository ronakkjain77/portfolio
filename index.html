01 A demand planning platform that plans past the forecast Iksula · multi-tenant SaaS · built end to end Platform

The problem

Most demand planning tools stop at a forecast and leave the buyer to work out what to actually order. The forecast is the easy half — the hard half is turning it into a purchase order with the right date, the right buffer and a quantity that fits on a truck.

What I did

  • Built the platform end to end on FastAPI, React and Docker, multi-tenant from the start, with a BRD and a repeatable client onboarding model.
  • Built segmentation and model-zoo backtesting that picks its own champion per series, with ABC/XYZ policies and accuracy/FVA tracking.
  • Extended past forecasting into purchase, distribution and production planning — lead-time-derived order dates, buffers sized from measured delivery variability, truck and case rounding, shelf-life gating, fair-share allocation.
  • Automated the painful part of onboarding: confidence-scored column auto-mapping, idempotent loads, and a readiness engine that quantifies the forecast-accuracy cost of every data gap.
  • Productised a 48-hour Diagnostic — three files in, data readiness and baseline accuracy out — seeding a Diagnostic → Planner → Intelligence ladder.

Outcome

A planning module measured on one honest trust metric: proposals released unedited. A human still issues every indent — the system has to earn the click, not assume it. Onboarding compressed into a 6-week, 8-session model.

02 Finding the money that quietly leaks out of a contract Iksula · Leakage & Recovery Accelerator · demo → production 7 scenarios

The problem

Contracts say what a fee, a deduction or a payment should be. Reality often disagrees, quietly and at scale. Finance teams find out months later, if at all — and a detection tool that stops at a flagged row has not recovered any cash.

What I did

  • Scaled a demo webapp into a shippable product for CFOs and finance teams: re-platformed MongoDB to PostgreSQL, rebuilt the UI on React 19, added CI and AWS ECS Fargate deployment behind a WAF.
  • Built a versioned no-code truth layer plus 10 deterministic evaluators that catch fees, deductions and payments breaching signed contract terms — deterministic, so a finding can be defended line by line.
  • Broadened the market beyond marketplace partners to 7 leakage scenarios — B2B short payments, overdue receivables, unearned discounts, AP duplicates and overpayments, SaaS overbilling, processing fees, royalty underpayment — packaged as a sellable Scenario Library.
  • Closed the loop from detection to recovered cash: escalation, dual-control approval, AI-drafted claim emails, follow-ups, recovery and write-off, with a full audit trail.
  • Shipped 6-role access control, so a junior analyst can flag and escalate but never act beyond their authority; multi-brand with a super-admin console.

Outcome

A product that goes all the way from "this invoice breaches the contract" to money actually back in the account — with the paper trail an auditor will ask for.

  • React 19
  • AWS ECS Fargate
  • CI/CD
  • Rules engines

03 Proving an SEO problem was a data problem Iksula · enterprise retail client · search readiness review 8.1% vs 19.5%

The problem

A large retailer was ranking for a lot of keywords and converting almost none of that into top-three positions. The instinct in the room was to rebuild the website. That is an expensive instinct to act on without evidence.

What I did

  • Scored 500 commercially-weighted products, drawn from a 13,678-product catalogue across 52 sub-categories, on 11 on-page parameters — using a scoring algorithm already validated on 100k+ products.
  • Benchmarked against live SERP data — real ranking keywords, real volumes, real positions — plus competitor page crawls, so the comparison was like-for-like rather than keyword guesswork.
  • Turned 500 rows into a per-product work queue naming the weakest link and the specific fix, rolled up to the 6 highest-payback fixes in order.
  • Delivered 3 Excel studies, a client deck and speaker notes, and scripted the pipeline so it re-runs across the full catalogue and measures movement after fixes land.

Outcome

The constraint came down to one line: 189,696 organic keywords, but only 8.1% of non-branded terms in Google's top 3 — against 19.5% for online-only rivals a fraction of the size. The bottleneck was product data, not site architecture. That reframed the whole remediation budget.

Free text became a queryable dimension. Performance scoring stopped being manual, and the dashboards finally reflected what people actually wrote.
